Seva First Innovation Challenge 2026 · Synopsis

WorkCheck Kachchh

Finding exposed workers through the public health system, and following every referral to completion

Problem statementPS000109: Digital Occupational Health Monitoring for Industrial Workers
Posted byDistrict Development Office, Kachchh (Healthtech)
TeamTM005024, LDRP Institute of Technology and Research, Gandhinagar
Kachchh's workers do not all appear on a factory register, but they do live in localities the public health system can reach. We add a short work and exposure check to existing screening, use heat and dust data to decide where to go first, and track each referral until it is completed. Required employer examinations are flagged, and workers keep their own record.

Requested outcome: “A secure digital system for periodic health screening, risk alerts and follow-up tracking.” The statement names labourers and localities as users, and lists four failures: separate records, missed periodic screening, untracked risks, and workers who change employers or locations.

1. The problem we are solving

The rules check workers by employer type and age. Dust and heat harm people by exposure. A worker can spend years in a hazardous job and still have no annual examination due.

RuleWho gets a free annual examination
Gujarat OSH Rules 2025, Rule 9Factory and building/construction workers who have completed 45, within 120 days of the start of the calendar year; Form 5 includes occupational history, chest X-ray and creatinine

Even where a duty exists, follow-through is weak:

  • Inspection is thin. Secondary summaries of national data put 2021 inspection coverage at about 15% of factories and 26% of hazardous factories.
  • Disease goes unrecorded. An ILO India article reports only 562 occupational disease cases reported nationally in 2011–16. In Morbi and Surendranagar, one group alone (PTRC) reports identifying 182 silicosis cases, including 80 deaths, since 2018. That is a case-finding count, not a prevalence estimate.
  • Workers have reason to stay silent. Sister statements PS000247 and PS000271 describe fear of job loss, and say that dust exposure “is rarely measured at an individual level”.

2. Our solution

1. PrioritiseHeat, job and screening data show which localities and work groups to reach first.
2. AskFrontline staff or a trained worker-volunteer complete a short work and exposure check.
3. RouteClinician-approved rules select the next step. Doctors make all medical decisions.
4. Follow upThe case stays open until attendance and any advised follow-up are recorded.
5. KeepThe worker holds a QR card and copies of doctor-issued results, across employers.

Where it plugs in. National programmes aim to screen everyone aged 30+ for common diseases at Ayushman Arogya Mandirs, using the CBAC checklist that also supports TB detection. We propose to add the work check alongside that process, subject to approval by the CDHO and DDO. Trained worker-volunteers and targeted camps would reach workers under 30 and remote salt-pan clusters.

The check asks about: current and past jobs, years in dust or heat, employer type, age, last examination, and symptoms. It is designed to take about a minute.

Three reasons a person can be routed for a check-up

RouteTriggerAction
Legal due dateRule 9 or Rule 5 applies to this workerFlag the exam as due, track the deadline, and record whether it happened. No alert is required.
Past or current exposureYears in a silica-risk or dusty job, or a repeated workplace dust flag; thresholds to be set by a clinicianReferral request to an agreed clinic. It never says “silicosis suspected”.
Symptoms or requestCough, breathlessness, repeated dark urine, or the worker asksDoctor review. Collapse or confusion in heat is an emergency: call 108 and start cooling.

Past exposure matters because silica harm is cumulative. A worker with 20 years at a stone crusher who now works on a clean site would never trigger a sensor alert, so sensor alerts cannot be the only route. In a Chinese multi-enterprise study, pneumoconiosis-like changes had about 3.3 times the odds with more than 10 years' exposure than with 5 or fewer (95% CI 1.14–9.69; small case count).

3. Where the sensor fits

The core system works without hardware. Low-cost sensors are an optional add-on, piloted at 2–3 sites where someone has agreed in advance what they will do when a reading is high.

Site node: what it is for

  • Heat: checks how far weather-based WBGT estimates are from local conditions in sun, shade and salt-pan glare.
  • Dust: compares jobs and shows whether controls such as water spraying are working. Weather and air-quality models cannot see dust at a crusher or loading point.
  • Local action: a lamp and SMS show job-specific work-rest advice, with water and shade instructions.

What it does not do

  • It does not measure silica, which needs lab analysis.
  • It does not diagnose. A dust flag only prompts dust control or a referral request.
  • It does not measure a worker's dose by itself. Site readings are combined with job and shift records.
  • It is not certified. In one construction dataset, two optical sensors differed by about 31 times during the same task, so each node must be checked against a reference.

4. What our Kachchh analysis already shows

We analysed hourly ERA5 and CAMS model data for five Kachchh locations. These are model estimates, not worksite measurements. Work-time allowances use the ACGIH-style criteria published by CCOHS for acclimatized workers. Workload classes are our assumption.

Job and location (2025 model)Assumed workloadAllowed work hours in a 9-hour shift, Apr–Oct
Port yard loading, Kandla, in sunHeavy2.3
Salt-pan raking, Little Rann, in sunHeavy2.5
Cement yard loading, Abdasa, in sunHeavy2.9
Outdoor construction, Bhuj, in sunHeavy3.1
Cement plant walk-around, Abdasa, in sunModerate4.9
Crane or truck cab, in shadeLight9.0
  • Job matters more than place. Under these assumptions, the same port can mean about 2.3 allowed hours for a yard loader and 9 for a shaded cab operator. Alerts must therefore be set by job, not only by site.
  • Heat is a long season. Kandla had an estimated 2,067 of 3,650 daytime hours in 2025 above 27.5 °C WBGT in sun, the level beyond which the table allows less than 75% work time for heavy work.
  • Shift timing helps. Moving a 9-hour shift from 9am–6pm to 6am–3pm avoids about 20% of those hot hours across the five sites (average of about 358 hours a year).
  • Related evidence. In a Tamil Nadu cohort of 352 salt-pan workers, 87.5% exceeded the heat limit, 28% had high post-shift urine specific gravity and 7% had eGFR below 60. No equivalent Kachchh study was found, which is why local validation is needed.

Public NCD screeningPopulation screening at 30+Work history is not its focus; we propose to add this layer

Lessons from elsewhere. Thailand delivered basic worker health services through primary care units and worker-volunteers. Brazil's experience shows frontline teams need training and referral support. South Africa's silicosis claims show how much records matter. A Nicaraguan sugar mill's experience suggests water, shade and rest changes may protect kidneys. Each is a different setting, so we will test these lessons locally.

6. Architecture, privacy and safeguards

Software: phone-first web app in Gujarati, Hindi and English; QR worker card; rules engine using a versioned, clinician-reviewed rule table; case tracker; SMS and voice alerts; FastAPI and SQL backend; district dashboard showing anonymous totals. Sensor readings and weather data feed the same system.

  • Consent first: taking part is voluntary, in the worker's language, and must not affect job, pay or care.
  • Worker control: employers see no individual results from our system without the worker's consent. Legal certificates remain the doctor's responsibility, because the rules also send them to the employer.
  • Clinical boundary: students and software do not diagnose; a clinician owns the rules and medical decisions.
  • Record integrity: examination status requires confirmation from the clinic, and the worker gets a copy.
  • Data rule: no synthetic or foreign data is presented as Kachchh measurement. Disease risk prediction stays out of scope until real outcome data exists.
