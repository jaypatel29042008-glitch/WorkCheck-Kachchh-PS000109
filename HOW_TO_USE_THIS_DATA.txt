1. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY & THE 3 CORE PITCH NUMBERS
================================================================================

Our Core Idea:
A low-cost solar site node (~Rs. 2,000) and dust badge (~Rs. 1,700-1,900) measure heat (WBGT) and dust (PM10/PM2.5).
When exposure breaches safe clinical limits:
  1. Worker receives a direct SMS alert in Gujarati/Hindi on their basic phone.
  2. The worker is added to a check-up priority list for existing services
     (BOCW, salt-worker van, ESIC); a clinician decides the tests.
  3. The health record is worker-owned and portable (tied to their e-Shram card).
  4. The DDO Kachchh accesses an anonymous district dashboard showing factory
     hotspots and heat stress compliance across ports, salt pans, and quarries.

THE 3 WINNING PITCH NUMBERS (Memorise these for judges):

1. A 6 AM START CUTS HEAT DANGER BY ~20% WITH THE SAME 9-HOUR SHIFT:
   - In 2025, standard 9 AM - 6 PM working hours exposed workers to 1,655 to 1,933
     dangerous heat hours (>27.5 °C WBGT).
   - Moving the same 9-hour shift to 6 AM - 3 PM avoids ~358 hazardous hours/year
     (19.9%, modelled). A 6 AM - 1 PM shift avoids ~816 hours (45.3%), but workers
     lose 2 hours a day, so it is not free.
   - System feature: DDO Dashboard generates automated seasonal shift advisories.

2. SAME WORKPLACE, VERY DIFFERENT DUST (INDIVIDUAL TRACKING):
   - In 29 of 56 multi-sample US OSHA inspections with a result over 50 ug/m3,
     another sample was under 25 ug/m3. The top 10% of samples hold 95.5% of measured
     silica (~80% without the 4 highest). Samples, not workers; 46.6% are recorded as 0.
   - Why it matters: A single environmental sensor measuring "average factory air"
     misses the workers standing over the concrete mixer or bagging chute.
     We must link exposure to individual worker e-Shram IDs based on job role.

3. THE INVISIBLE COASTAL TRAP & THE "NO-SLEEP" HEAT DEBT:
   - At Kandla Port and Mundra Port, summer air temperatures look mild (~31-34 °C)
     compared to inland desert heat (~43 °C). But coastal humidity (65-69%) drives
     WBGT past the danger limit for 2,067 hours a year.
   - Worse: 54.8% of dangerous days are followed by tropical nights (WBGT >= 25 °C),
     preventing sleep recovery. Our algorithm carries over "heat debt" to the next day.

FOR NEEL (DATABASE & RULES ENGINE):
Files to read: `db/README.md`, `data/processed/insights.csv`

1. Ingestion Payload Schema (JSON over MQTT/HTTP):
   {
     "device_id": "kandla-berth-04",
     "time": "2026-11-05T13:30:00+05:30",
     "temp_c": 34.2,
     "humidity_rh": 68.0,
     "globe_temp_c": 49.5,
     "wind_ms": 2.1,
     "pm10": 145.0,
     "pm25": 72.0
   }

2. Table Definitions to Build in `db/`:
   - `sites`: id, name, category (port, salt_pan, cement, mining), lat, lon
   - `devices`: id, site_id, is_replay, last_seen, calibration_factor
   - `workers`: id, eshram_id, phone, name, trade, site_id, preferred_lang
   - `readings`: raw 1-minute telemetry
   - `daily_exposure`: rollups of hours over 27.5 C, max WBGT, 8-h TWA dust
   - `rules`: threshold configurations (editable via admin, never hardcoded)
   - `alerts`: triggered SMS messages (timestamp, worker_id, hazard_type, text)
   - `checkup_cases`: booked health visits (status: booked, attended, completed)

3. SQL Rules Engine Thresholds:
   - WBGT Heavy Work Warning: WBGT_sun >= 26.0 °C (Hydration prompt SMS)
   - WBGT Heavy Work Action:  WBGT_sun >= 27.5 °C (Mandatory rest cycle prompt)
   - WBGT Moderate Work:      WBGT_sun >= 28.0 °C
   - Dust 15-min STEL Alert:  Rolling 15-min PM10 >= 300 µg/m³
   - Dust 8-hr TWA Action:    Silica estimate >= 50 ug/m3 (warning 25) with a lab-tested site silica %;
                              otherwise PM10 >= 100 ug/m3 as a screening flag (our choice, from an
                              outdoor standard, not a workplace limit). Adds worker to priority list.
   - Sensor Glitch Filter:    Discard any single-minute PM10 > 10,000 µg/m³
   - Cumulative Heat Debt:    If previous night WBGT >= 25.0 °C, reduce daytime
                              trigger threshold by 1.0 °C (to 26.5 °C WBGT).

FOR PERSON 2 (WOKWI DEVICE MOCK-UP & HARDWARE CODE):
Files to read: `scripts/wbgt_rebuild.py`, `charts/mendeley_dust_trace.png`

1. Simulated Hardware Components:
   - ESP32 Microcontroller (Wi-Fi + BLE + Deep Sleep)
   - DHT22 or SHT31 (Air Temperature & Relative Humidity)
   - DS18B20 inside 150 mm matte black copper sphere (Black Globe Temp Tg)
   - Plantower PMS5003 or GP2Y1010 (Optical Particulate Matter PM10/PM2.5)
   - Anemometer pulse counter or fixed 1.0 m/s default wind model

2. Firmware Algorithm in C++/MicroPython:
   - Step 1: Read sensors every 2 seconds.
   - Step 2: Sanity check: If PM10 > 10,000 µg/m³, drop as electrical glitch (NIOSH 2024 rule).
   - Step 3: Compute psychrometric wet bulb and Bernard natural wet bulb:
       Tnwb = Tpwb + 0.25*(Tg - Ta) + 0.1/(v^1.1) - 0.2
   - Step 4: Compute site WBGT:
       WBGT = 0.7*Tnwb + 0.2*Tg + 0.1*Ta
   - Step 5: Maintain a 15-minute rolling FIFO ring buffer (450 samples) for STEL.
   - Step 6: Transmit 1-minute average telemetry to Neel's ingestion API.

3. Hardware Cost Model (Bill of Materials):
   - ESP32 NodeMCU:          Rs. 450 (~$5.50)
   - SHT31 Temp/RH:          Rs. 250 (~$3.00)
   - DS18B20 + Copper Globe: Rs. 350 (~$4.20)
   - Solar Panel (5W) + TP4056 + 18650 Li-ion battery: Rs. 600 (~$7.20)
   - Enclosure & PCB:        Rs. 300 (~$3.60)
   - SITE NODE TOTAL:        About Rs. 2,000
   - DUST BADGE:             ESP32 Rs. 450 + PMS5003 Rs. 950 + battery/enclosure Rs. 300-500
                             = about Rs. 1,700-1,900 (synopsis: Rs. 1,500, the low end of its prices)
   Retail estimates, not quotes; parts still need calibration.
   NIOSH 2024: <$300 low-cost packages vs ~$5,000 commercial monitors (not our parts).

5. SCIENTIFIC & TECHNICAL CONCEPTS CHEATSHEET
================================================================================

- WBGT (Wet Bulb Globe Temperature):
  Standard: ISO 7243.
  Formula: 0.7*Tnwb + 0.2*Tg + 0.1*Ta (outdoor sun); 0.7*Tnwb + 0.3*Tg (shade).
  Why: Air temp alone ignores sweat evaporation and solar radiation load.

- ACGIH TLV Limits (Continuous Heavy Work):
  27.5 °C WBGT for heavy labour (shovelling salt, handling coal, quarrying).
  28.0 °C WBGT for moderate labour.

- Optical Dust Photometry:
  Light scattering detects particle count; does NOT weigh mass.
  Requires lab co-located gravimetric filter calibration (NIOSH 2024).

- Spirometry (Lung Function Testing):
  FEV1 (Forced Expiratory Volume in 1 sec) / FVC (Forced Vital Capacity).
  Ratio < 0.70 signifies obstructive pulmonary damage.
  Workers with high dust have 2.46x higher odds of abnormal ratio (He et al. 2022).

- Exposure Variation Across Samples:
  Top 10% of OSHA samples hold 95.5% of measured silica. Do not quote the Gini: zeros inflate it.

6. HONESTY & DEFENSE RULES FOR JUDGES' QUESTIONS
================================================================================

When judges probe the data, use these exact honest responses:

Q: "Did you measure this in Kachchh yourselves?"
A: "No. These are atmospheric models from Open-Meteo (ERA5 weather reanalysis and
    CAMS air quality) at 30-40 km grid resolution. They prove the macro-environment
    is hazardous, but worksite microclimates (salt albedo, quarry dust) will have
    higher localized extremes. That is exactly why physical IoT nodes are needed."

Q: "Why did you use US OSHA data for silica?"
A: "We did not find an open Indian dataset of personal worker silica samples. OSHA's
    599 US samples show results differ widely inside one workplace: in 29 of 56
    multi-sample inspections with a result over the limit, another was under the action
    level. It supports individual tracking but says nothing about Kachchh levels."

Q: "Can't employers just build a shade roof?"
A: "Our data proves shade drops WBGT by ~2.7 °C and solves ~59% of danger hours,
    but high coastal humidity leaves 600 to 997 hours dangerous even in full shade.
    Cooling requires airflow fans, electrolyte hydration, and shift rescheduling."

Q: "Why not alert on 8-hour TWA?"
A: "Mendeley construction data proves that short acute bursts (like 16 minutes of
    dry concrete mixing) get diluted by 30x in an 8-hour average. We use a 15-minute
    STEL for immediate worker protection and 8-hour TWA for doctor check-ups."
